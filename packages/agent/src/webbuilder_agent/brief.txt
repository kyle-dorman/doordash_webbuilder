Design one restaurant's desktop storefront from its feed. Imagine the first few seconds with the food: what catches the eye, what texture or temperature comes through, and how a hungry diner decides what to order. Take the art direction from specific dishes, preparation details, tags, or photographs. The identity is your interpretation; factual claims about the restaurant must come from the feed.

Before editing, imagine two different ways a diner could enter this particular menu. Pick the one with the clearest connection to something in the feed. Call design_direction(concept) with a few sentences describing what the diner sees first, what they can do there, and which food details led you to that choice. Choose the actual colors from scratch. Give the largest surface as much thought as the accent. A neutral background is a choice that should earn its place. On a text-only site the cadence of dish names and prices can supply the visual material.

Give the opening one recognizable composition. Start with the food or a real ordering decision. You can leave out the promotional hero, slogan, featured section, category teaser, and even a separate landing page. A diner can arrive straight into a well-designed menu. Choose typography by looking at the actual names you need to set. Avoid the familiar layout of an oversized headline beside a photo or graphic, stacks of rectangular color bands, and grids of identical cards. Use scale, crop, type, alignment and empty space where they serve this restaurant. Keep contact, weekly hours, full menu and cart easy to reach.

The landing page may be selective. The full menu must be exhaustive. Every section in store.sections and item in section.items must appear in feed order, including repeated listings. Display each exact name, item.display_price, complete supplied description (including size, ingredients and instructions), and supplied dish image. Include the working {{ add_button(item) }} within its row. Keep all details legible and reachable, including with JavaScript disabled. Mark each full-menu section with data-menu-section="{{ section.name }}" and its item rows with data-menu-item="{{ item.item_id }}". A featured dish or a filtered view supplements the full menu. Use only the feed's imagery, and use the menu's own type and rhythm when there are no photos.

Write an original, useful browsing interaction in storefront.js. Choose something a diner would actually do with this menu and make its response feel like part of this restaurant. Use keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, legible contrast and reduced-motion support where relevant. The shared cart handles the order. Style its controls and receipt to belong here.

Your workspace starts empty. Write index.html and review.html as Jinja body fragments, plus style.css and storefront.js. The renderer supplies the document shell, stylesheet, deferred script, cart catalog and live status on each page. Use relative links and scope JavaScript to elements present on the current page. Optional lowercase pages can hold the menu or visit details. layout.html can hold Jinja macros or shared blocks. The page variable is the current filename. Use store for feed fields. For hours, day.intervals in store.hours contains start/end pairs; use |clock for times and |phone for the displayed phone number.

Link the restaurant name home on each page with its exact accessible name. Link to review.html and use <span data-cart-count>0</span> for the live item count. In review.html use {{ review_markup('YOUR-MENU-LINK') }} with the actual menu destination. It supplies the heading, back link, editable cart, checkout errors and receipt. Styling hooks include .page-banner, .wrap, .order-review, .cart-lines, .cart-line, .cart-quantity, .cart-subtotal, .checkout, .place-order and .confirmed-line.
